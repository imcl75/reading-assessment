## 24-7 Nellie Bly (Round the World in Seventy-Two Days)
- Reporter for the New York World; proposed the trip to her editor in 1888; inspired by Jules Verne's Around the World in Eighty Days (Phileas Fogg, eighty days): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_the_World_in_Seventy-Two_Days ; https://www.heinzhistorycenter.org/learn/women-forging-the-way/nellie-bly-around-the-world/
- Left Hoboken on the 14th of November 1889 on the steamship Augusta Victoria; one small bag/satchel: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_the_World_in_Seventy-Two_Days ; https://www.hobokengirl.com/around-the-world-in-80-days-hoboken-jersey-city-nellie-bly/
- Crossing took 8 days (14th-22nd November), Southampton, then Amiens to meet Jules Verne; he said he would applaud if she did it in 79 days: Wikipedia page above; https://lamulnix.com/stories/Nellie-Bly-Around-the-World-in-Seventy-Two-Days-Meeting-Jules-Verne/ ; https://nelliebly125.wordpress.com/2014/09/08/in-which-nellie-meets-jules-verne/
- Route: Brindisi, Port Said/Suez Canal, Colombo (8th Dec), Singapore (bought a monkey), Hong Kong, Yokohama, Pacific crossing 14 days (7th-21st Jan), special train: Wikipedia page above; Smithsonian https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/nellie-blys-record-breaking-trip-around-world-was-to-her-surprise-race-180957910/
- Pulitzer's paper arranged a special train from San Francisco; arrived Jersey City 3:51 pm, the 25th of January 1890; 72 days 6 hours 11 minutes; about 25,000 miles (24,899): Wikipedia page above; https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/films/world
- Elizabeth Bisland (magazine, opposite direction), Nellie beat her by about four days and did not know of the race at the time: Smithsonian link above.
- LEFT OUT: leper colony visit (illness); exact times of day on departure.

## 25-7 Ellen MacArthur
- Born 8th July 1976, Whatstandwell, Derbyshire: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_MacArthur ; Britannica https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ellen-MacArthur
- First sailing as a young child on aunt's boat on east coast of England (age stated as about four in one source, so text says "young child"): Wikipedia; https://kids.kiddle.co/Ellen_MacArthur
- Saved money for about three years to buy an eight-foot dinghy (only Wikipedia-derived sources; hedged as "for years" and "small"): Wikipedia; https://culture.fandom.com/wiki/Ellen_MacArthur
- Finished second in the Vendee Globe, 2001, aged 24, youngest to complete it: Wikipedia; https://sailmagazine.com/web-exclusives/womens-history-spotlight-dame-ellen-macarthur/
- 2005: set off Nov 2004 from Falmouth, trimaran B&Q, 71 days 14 h 18 min 33 s, record broken by over a day (1 d 8 h 35 min), arrived 7th Feb 2005 to crowds in Falmouth aged 28: https://www.countypress.co.uk/news/23302283.day-2005-ellen-macarthur-smashed-round-world-sailing-record/ ; https://en.mercopress.com/2005/02/08/ellen-macarthur-beats-world-record ; Wikipedia
- Made Dame Commander (youngest at the time) soon after: Wikipedia; Britannica; Mercopress
- Ellen MacArthur Cancer Trust set up 2003, takes young people sailing to rebuild confidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_MacArthur_Cancer_Trust ; https://www.ellenmacarthurcancertrust.org/about/
- Foundation launched 2nd Sept 2010, aim circular economy (using things again, less waste): https://www.ellenmacarthurfoundation.org/ ; https://www.weforum.org/organizations/ellen-macarthur-foundation/
- LEFT OUT: storms/danger, boat name Threep'ny Bit, illness wording ("cancer") kept to charity name only is avoided in text.

## 26-7 Junko Tabei
- Born 22nd September 1939, Miharu, Japan; fifth of seven children: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junko_Tabei ; https://womeninexploration.org/timeline/junko-tabei/
- Aged ten, class trip to Mount Nasu began love of climbing: Wikipedia; CNN https://www.cnn.com/2025/05/14/travel/junko-tabei-first-woman-climb-everest ; https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/junko-tabei
- Studied English and American literature at Showa Women's University; joined men's clubs: Wikipedia; https://wanderwomenproject.com/women/junko-tabei/
- Founded Ladies Climbing Club 1969, motto "Let's go on an overseas expedition by ourselves": Wikipedia; https://www.hipcamp.com/journal/camping/women-who-wild-junko-tabei
- 1975 expedition of 15 women; sponsors Yomiuri Shimbun newspaper and Nippon Television, still had to raise money; she taught piano; team made their own clothing (no women's sizes): Wikipedia; Hipcamp; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/first-woman-to-climb-everest-junko-tabei-dies
- 16th May 1975 reached Everest summit with Sherpa Ang Tsering, first woman: Wikipedia; CNN; https://ebnw.net/empowerment/success-story/junko-tabei-everest-summit-first-woman-1975/
- 1992 first woman to climb highest peak on every continent (Seven Summits): Wikipedia; National Geographic
- Later environmental work on mountains: Wikipedia
- LEFT OUT: the avalanche, the piano-to-curtain details (only one source), age 35 (mentioned as computed; she was 35 on 16 May 1975, dob 22 Sept 1939 - omitted to be safe), death.
