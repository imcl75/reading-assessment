## 27-7 Nurse, Traveller and Business Owner (Mary Seacole)
- Born Kingston, Jamaica, 1805 (day not used; sources give varied detail): https://www.britannica.com/biography/Mary-Seacole (search summary), https://www.natgeokids.com/uk/discover/history/general-history/mary-seacole/
- Father a Scottish soldier; mother a healer using herbs/traditional remedies who ran a boarding house: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/mary-seacole-doctress-crimean-war/ ; https://www.natgeokids.com/uk/discover/history/general-history/mary-seacole/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Seacole
- Visited England when young; travelled to the Bahamas, Haiti, Cuba; bought spices, pickles, jams to sell in Jamaica: https://www.natgeokids.com/uk/discover/history/general-history/mary-seacole/
- Panama 1851, restaurant at half-brother's hotel, dispensed medicines: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/mary-seacole-doctress-crimean-war/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Seacole
- Offer to work as a nurse turned down; self-funded trip to the Crimea in 1855 with Thomas Day: London Museum page; NatGeo Kids page
- British Hotel near Balaclava: hut of metal sheets, soldiers could rest and buy hot food, drinks, equipment; storehouse/canteen/resting place: NatGeo Kids; London Museum; https://www.maryseacoletrust.org.uk/learn-about-mary/ (search result)
- Nickname "Mother Seacole": NatGeo Kids; Wikipedia
- Wonderful Adventures of Mrs Seacole in Many Lands, 1857, bestseller; first autobiography by a Black woman in Britain: London Museum; Wikipedia; Britannica (search summary)
- Name faded, rediscovered later (hedged as "not widely remembered"): NatGeo Kids
- Voted greatest Black Briton, 2004 (100 Great Black Britons poll): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Great_Black_Britons ; Wikipedia
- Statue in gardens of St Thomas' Hospital unveiled 30 June 2016 (year only used): https://www.london-se1.co.uk/news/view/8807 ; https://www.nursingtimes.net/news/charities/long-awaited-mary-seacole-statue-is-unveiled-30-06-2016/
- Left out: exact birth date (sources vary/unsure), the Crimean War by name, illness, battlefields, death, and the Nightingale team refusal detail. Crimea described only as a region beside the Black Sea.

## 28-7 The Doctor Who Went to Space (Mae Jemison)
- Endeavour launched 12 September 1992, seven astronauts (Jemison + six), first African American woman in space: https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/she-had-dream-mae-c-jemison-first-african-american-woman-space ; https://www.womenshistory.org/education-resources/biographies/mae-jemison ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Mae-Jemison ; https://www.nasa.gov/former-astronaut-mae-c-jemison/
- Born Alabama 1956, moved to Chicago as a young child; parents encouraged curiosity, museums and library books: womenshistory.org page
- Started Stanford at 16; degrees chemical engineering and African American studies; medical degree Cornell (1981): NASM page; womenshistory.org
- Peace Corps medical officer in Sierra Leone and Liberia, "around two years" (sources say two to two and a half): NASM; womenshistory.org; Space.com (search summary)
- One of 15 chosen from about 2,000 applicants (year omitted: sources give 1987/1988): NASM; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_12 (search result)
- Mission specialist; mission nearly eight days (12th-20th September 1992); more than 40 experiments (44 reported); bone cell research, frog eggs, motion sickness: NASA STS-47 page https://www.nasa.gov/mission_pages/shuttle/shuttlemissions/archives/sts-47.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-47 ; NASM page
- Left NASA 1993; taught at Dartmouth College; The Earth We Share science camp (1994) for young people; 100 Year Starship: Britannica; https://montgomery.dartmouth.edu/people/mae-c-jemison ; womenshistory.org
- Left out: exact orbit count (126/127 differ), exact birth date, 1987 vs 1988 selection year.

## 29-7 A Licence Earned in France (Bessie Coleman)
- Born 26 January 1892 in Texas (town omitted: Atlanta vs Waxahachie differ); one of thirteen children: https://www.chicagohistory.org/bessie-coleman-aviation-pioneer/ ; https://www.womenshistory.org/education-resources/biographies/bessie-coleman ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessie_Coleman
- Moved to Chicago 1915 to find better opportunities, living with two brothers; manicurist: Chicago History Museum; https://airandspace.si.edu/explore/stories/bessie-coleman
- Brother's stories of French women flyers inspired her: NASM page; https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/bessie-coleman (search summary)
- US flight schools refused her because of race and gender: NASM; womenshistory.org
- Robert S. Abbott of the Chicago Defender advised France; she learned French (Berlitz class, Chicago), saved money: womenshistory.org; Chicago History Museum; Wikipedia
- Sailed November 1920 (20th) to France; Caudron Brothers' School of Aviation, Le Crotoy; Nieuport biplane with stick and rudder bar: Wikipedia; NASM; Chicago History Museum
- Licence 15 June 1921 from the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale; first African American woman to earn a pilot's licence; "in seven months": NASM; womenshistory.org; Chicago History Museum
- Returned to New York September 1921, greeted by reporters: NASM; womenshistory.org
- Flying displays/barnstorming; loops, figure eights; refused to perform where Black people were not allowed to attend: NASM; womenshistory.org; Wikipedia
- Left out: her death, exact first-show dates and crowd numbers, the "first Native American" claim.
