## 30-7 The Girl Who Checked the Numbers (Katherine Johnson)
- Born 26 Aug 1918, White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia: NASA bio https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/langley/katherine-johnson-biography/ ; Wikipedia.
- "I counted everything..." quote; computers did tedious precise calculating by hand: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/langley/katherine-johnson-the-girl-who-loved-to-count/
- Joined Langley June 1953; worked 33 years, retired 1986: NASA bio.
- Separate West Area Computing section for Black women (kept to one calm sentence): NASA bio; Wikipedia.
- Trajectory for Alan Shepard, 5 May 1961 (first American in space): NASA bio; Wikipedia.
- 1962: Glenn asked her to recheck electronic computers' numbers by hand; flight 20 Feb 1962, first American to orbit: NASA pages; Wikipedia.
- Apollo (incl. Apollo 11, 1969) and Space Shuttle work: NASA pages.
- Medal of Freedom 24 Nov 2015, age 97, from Obama; highest civilian honour: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/langley/former-nasa-langley-mathematician-to-be-awarded-presidential-medal-of-freedom/
- Encouraged students in STEM: Wikipedia.
- LEFT OUT: age she started college (sources differ: NASA bio says 18); Glenn "if she says they're good" quote (not confirmed).

## 31-7 The Doctor of Aldeburgh (Elizabeth Garrett Anderson)
- Born 9 June 1836 in London (Whitechapel): Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Garrett_Anderson
- Nurse at Middlesex Hospital 1860; private tuition in Latin, Greek, anatomy etc.; medical schools refused: Wikipedia.
- Society of Apothecaries licence 1865, first woman qualified in Britain; society then changed rules: Wikipedia; UCL https://www.ucl.ac.uk/about/search-faces-ucl/exposing-loophole-elizabeth-garrett-anderson-britains-first-female-doctor (via search summary)
- 1866 St Mary's Dispensary for Women and Children: Wikipedia; search summaries.
- 1874 co-founded London School of Medicine for Women; dean until 1902: Wikipedia.
- Married James George Skelton Anderson 1871; retired to Aldeburgh 1902; mayor 1908, first woman mayor in England: Wikipedia.
- LEFT OUT: childhood in Aldeburgh, dispensary growing into a hospital (not confirmed here), Sorbonne MD.

## 32-7 A Prize from Sweet Wormwood (Tu Youyou)
- Born 30 Dec 1930, Ningbo: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu_Youyou ; search summaries (Nobel/IBSA).
- Project 523; malaria drugs losing effect (chloroquine-resistant): Wikipedia.
- Team head from 1969 (search summaries); by 1971 over 2,000 recipes screened, 380 extracts from about 200 plants, tested in mice: Wikipedia.
- Ge Hong text about 1,600 years old, cold-water soaking, heat destroys the ingredient, low-temperature extraction: Wikipedia; IBSA https://www.ibsafoundation.org/en/blog/tu-youyou-the-nobel-prize-for-her-work-on-malaria
- 1972 pure substance isolated, named qinghaosu / artemisinin: Wikipedia.
- 2011 Lasker-DeBakey award, first Chinese recipient: Wikipedia; search.
- Nobel Prize Physiology or Medicine 2015, shared with Campbell and Omura; first woman from mainland China to win a Nobel Prize: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2015/tu/facts/ ; Wikipedia.
- LEFT OUT: her childhood illness, volunteering to test the medicine, university details, exact 1969 leadership wording kept as "asked to lead a research team".
