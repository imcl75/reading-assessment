## 32-8 Stephen Hawking: Asking the Biggest Questions
- Born 8th January 1942 in Oxford; physics at University College, Oxford (first-class honours). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking ; https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/stephen-hawking ; EBSCO Research Starters https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/stephen-hawking/
- Moved to Cambridge October 1962 for graduate work; PhD 1966 (Trinity Hall). Sources: Wikipedia; EBSCO; Cambridge (arrived 1962).
- Fellow of Gonville and Caius throughout life. Source: https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/stephen-hawking
- Black hole definition (gravity so strong nothing, not even light, can escape): https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/
- 1974: black holes produce radiation (Hawking radiation); could evaporate/fade. Sources: Cambridge; EBSCO.
- Fellow of the Royal Society 1974: Wikipedia. Lucasian Professor 1979, post once held by Isaac Newton: Cambridge; EBSCO.
- CBE 1982; Copley Medal; Albert Einstein Award; Presidential Medal of Freedom 2009: Cambridge page; Wikipedia (2009).
- Wheelchair; speech-generating device, first handheld switch, later a single cheek muscle; recognisable computerised voice: EBSCO; Cambridge.
- A Brief History of Time 1988, surprise bestseller, 237 weeks on Sunday Times list: Cambridge; Wikipedia. "Millions of copies" (Wikipedia: about 9 million; hedged).
- The Universe in a Nutshell 2001; A Briefer History of Time 2005 with Leonard Mlodinow: Wikipedia.
- Left out: illness name/diagnosis, death, personal life.

## 33-7 A Letter to Cambridge (Ramanujan)
- Born 22nd December 1887, Erode: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srinivasa_Ramanujan ; grew up in Kumbakonam (mother moved him at age one), Town High School January 1898, Carr's Synopsis found there ("theorems, formulae and short proofs"): MacTutor https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Ramanujan/
- Taught himself from Carr; notebooks; nearly 3,900 results, many new: Wikipedia; MacTutor (notebooks still studied).
- Clerk at Madras Port Trust from 1912: Wikipedia; MacTutor.
- Wrote to Hardy January 1913 with unproved theorems; "no university education but ordinary school course": MacTutor.
- Hardy studied it with Littlewood; reply 8th February 1913 with three categories: MacTutor.
- Sailed 17th March 1914, arrived London 14th April 1914, Trinity rooms 30th April: MacTutor.
- BA by Research 1916; FRS 1918 (confirmed May 1918, age 31); Fellow of Trinity 10th October 1918, first Indian: Wikipedia; MacTutor.
- 1729 taxi story, recorded by Hardy (1940 book): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1729_(number) ; MathWorld. Hospital setting and "dull" wording softened/omitted.
- Left out: illness, return to India, death.

## 34-7 Planting an Idea (Wangari Maathai)
- Born 1st April 1940 near Nyeri, Kenya: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wangari_Maathai ; Green Belt Movement site https://www.greenbeltmovement.org/wangari-maathai ; Marginalian.
- Biology at Mount St. Scholastica College, Kansas; master's Pittsburgh; PhD Nairobi 1971, first woman in East and Central Africa with a doctorate: Wikipedia; Green Belt Movement site.
- Problems seen (deforestation, soil loss, streams drying, firewood scarce, food less reliable), 1977 founding, women encouraged to plant native tree seedlings: Nobel Prize summary via search results; Wikipedia.
- Women given modest compensation; trained in forestry, food processing, bee-keeping; over 30,000 women: Wikipedia; https://www.learningtogive.org/resources/wangari-maathai-and-green-belt-movement
- Over 51 million trees planted since 1977: Green Belt Movement / Wikipedia Green Belt Movement page.
- Trees address soil erosion, water scarcity, fuel shortage: Wikipedia Green Belt Movement page. Statement that roots hold soil in place is general established science.
- Nobel Peace Prize 2004, first African woman, first environmentalist: Wikipedia.
- Left out: arrests, politics, illness, death.

## 34-8 Learning the Law (Nelson Mandela)
- Born 18th July 1918, Mvezo, Eastern Cape; teacher gave name Nelson at primary school in Qunu: https://www.nelsonmandela.org/biography ; Wikipedia.
- Clarkebury, Healdtown, Fort Hare; BA through University of South Africa 1943; law at University of the Witwatersrand: nelsonmandela.org; Wikipedia.
- Mandela and Tambo, opened 1952 in Johannesburg, Chancellor House, Ferreirasdorp, first firm run by Black partners, two small rooms, Tambo paperwork and Mandela in magistrates' courts, clients seeking redress, restored and reopened 2011 as museum/archive: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandela_and_Tambo
- 27 years in prison, released 11th February 1990: nelsonmandela.org.
- Nobel Peace Prize 1993 shared with F. W. de Klerk; more than 250 honours: Wikipedia.
- 1994 election, first with people of all races (voting dates given differently, so "April 1994" only); inaugurated 10th May 1994, Union Buildings Pretoria, thousands attended; served to 1999, declined a second term: nelsonmandela.org; History.com; Wikipedia.
- Left out: Fort Hare expulsion, political campaigns, trial, imprisonment details, death.
