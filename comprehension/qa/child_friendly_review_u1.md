## Found and changed (patch file: qa_patches_u1.py)
- 21-5 Lantern Night: "At eight o'clock" (a clock time) became "After it got dark". No question depended on it.
- 20-6 Diwali: "in October or November" (months) became "when evenings get dark early". Q2 (season = autumn) still works, as the text still says autumn.
- 22-1 The New Girl: "backpack" (American) became "rucksack" in the text (two places) and in Q11.
- 19-5 Bird Feeder: "solid vegetable fat, called suet or lard" was wrong (lard is animal fat) and adult jargon. Now "solid fat for birds, called suet".
- 20-2 Owls: "voles" (unfamiliar animal) became "worms".
- 20-4 Flatbreads: "hummus" became "a sauce".
- 21-1 Bookshop: "second-hand bookshop" became "bookshop for old books".
- 22-4 Zip: "more than a hundred years ago" became "a very long time ago".
- 22-5 Sports Day: "simultaneously" became "both".

## Left alone and why
- Durations (twenty minutes, half an hour, fifteen minutes, five days): a child can picture these; they are not clock times. Several questions test them (19-5 Q1, 20-4 Q5/Q10).
- Days of the week and seasons: everyday cues.
- Idioms and similes that a question tests directly (butterflies in the tummy, ten feet tall, melted away, head to toe, as red as apples, hearts pumping, burst with pride, the saying about bad weather): the question gives support and it is the skill being assessed.
- Words tested as vocabulary (poking out, crept, kneads, fermenting, interlocking, temporary, habitat, nymph, talons, limpets, anemones): kept.
- Level 22 stretch vocabulary (intently, unfamiliar, concealed, prised, sportsmanship): appropriate for the top level and inferable from context.
- Places and foods (Nigeria, Kenya, Portugal, cacao, diyas, rangoli): named on purpose and explained or asked about in context.
- No other Americanisms, dates, years or adult workplaces were found. Earlier gender-balance fixes are untouched.
