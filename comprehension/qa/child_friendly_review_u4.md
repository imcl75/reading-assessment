# Child-friendly review, set u4 (18 comprehension tests, levels 32-34; 19 fluency passages, levels 18-36)

## Result
No clear problems found, so nothing was changed. qa_patches_u4.py is empty and child_friendly_fluency_edits.json is [].

## What was checked
- Americanisms: none. UK forms throughout (torch, duvet, launderette, chips, programme, Grandad, jumper-type words, pounds, "Mr").
- Clock times and numbers, fluency 18-24 (strict): none. Times are already concrete (Saturday morning, at night, in winter, before dawn, by midday, all week). Nothing to replace.
- Clock times, comprehension (light touch): "at nine o'clock" (33-5), "at seven that morning" (33-1) and "At half past four" (34-5, also set by the title "Before Dawn") are scene-setting for 9-11 year-olds who read clock times. No question depends on reading them, so left alone.
- Dates and numbers: kept where the topic needs them (Rosetta Stone 1799/1822, Gutenberg c.1450, Bell 1967, Ada 1815/1842, Hadrian's Wall AD 122 and 73 miles). Answerable from the text.
- Background knowledge: technical terms are defined in the text (hibernation, magma/lava, dormancy, enzymes, plucking, abrasion, milecastle, pulsar, peat). Chess, sunflower shows, bookbinding, papermaking, weaving and stadiums are all explained by context.
- Cultural references: UK-wide or explained (village show, allotment, Sports Day-type events, ice cream, waggle dance). Nothing that needs adult explanation.
- Gender-balance fixes untouched.

## Left alone deliberately
Challenging vocabulary that sets the level (reprimand, brazen, exempt, antimony, dormancy, scholarship, gavel, auctioneer) is what makes these levels harder, and each is inferable from context or is a vocabulary question target. Changing it would lower difficulty, not remove an unfriendly feature.
