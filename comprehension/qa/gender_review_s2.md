## Left alone, and why
- 25-3 (Victorian school): girls did needlework and boys did drill or drawing. This is a historical fact, and the text only reports it. Kept.
- 26-6 (Braille), 27-5 (penicillin), 23-4 (Foley): the people are male because the facts are. Wording is neutral and no stereotype is given. 23-6 (Mary Anning) already names the sexism she faced. Set is dominated by male real people (6 to 1); nothing to change without altering facts.
- 26-3: Po Po remains the family dumpling expert. She is a named grandmother in a culturally specific family tradition and the whole family (aunts, uncles, cousins) cooks. Changing her to a grandfather would need many edits for little gain. Noted for the teacher to consider.
- 27-3 (ballet shoes, scarves): they belong to Nana Joy, a dancer, so this is character detail. The narrator is unnamed and neutral.
- 24-2: fairy-tale inversion of "The Princess and the Pea" (the Queen chooses, Nell mends carts, the Prince laughs). Dot the maid is a small named role balanced by strong female and male characters. Kept.
- 24-4: Poppy makes the rules but Yusuf argues equally, and Zara is competent. Not gendered. Kept.
- 26-4 (orangutans): "an adult male can stretch his arms" and mothers raising babies are biological facts.
- 27-2: Brownies is a girls' organisation, factual. Kept.
- Kai's nerves (25-1) and Idris's fear (25-6): fearful boys, so no reinforcing of the stereotype.
